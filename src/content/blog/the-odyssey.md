I recently went to the theatres to watch the Odyssey by Chirstopher Nolan. Funnily enough, this was the first Nolan film I watched in cinema, as Nolan intended. The acting, the direction, the music, each and every creative design element was perfect in my opinion. But that’s not what’s coming back to me every single day after coming back from the theatres.

The story of Odysseus, and his journey home, is quite literally older than history. The poet Homer penned it to papyrus around 3000 years ago. But by word of mouth, it has been alive for far longer. However, the way Nolan and his brilliant team of artists has portrayed it on film has left an indelible mark on me. What is the soul of a man?

Odysseus faced war for 10 years, devised the Trojan horse and saw the siege of Troy with his own eyes. He saw merciless killing, pillaging, maiming and every sin imaginable and more. Yet did not care to evaluate his actions as a general. He was a cunning warrior. Perhaps not the best Greek, but one of the finest. But with all his wisdom, he could not recognize that none of his actions during the war aligned with that of a virtuous general.

Throughout the movie, there is only one tenet that is set in stone for each Greek and that is Zeus’s law. And the greatest violation of that was the Trojan horse and its creator Odysseus. From the moment his men slayed the priestess of Athena, the entire pantheon unleashed the Aegean sea on his crew. Because he violated, in the most vilest way, the sacred law of the land.

He and his men faced monsters, sorcerers and so much more. But, none of this changed him inside. Ludwig played this out beautifully when during the pass by the sirens, Odysseus heard their songs. It was the same song that played when he reunited with Penelope. And so, deep down, Odysseus only wanted to get home to his wife and live his days out peacefully on Ithaca.

Maybe that’s the soul of a man, no matter how many violations he commits, he retains the only thing in his control. That star in the sky he chases. Which rises above all else, no matter the cost.
