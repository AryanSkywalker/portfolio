It’s been a long time since I penned one of these. To be fair, it has also been about as long since I’ve had an epiphany. But this one’s not so apparent as it is slow and enduring.

Recently, videos of Anthony Bourdain have been making a comeback on the internet. And while I’ve heard him talk many times before, there have been things he says that I’ve only now come to accept as true. Bored, or rather ‘tired’ from working long hours in Les Halles in Manhattan, he finally came around to doing the stuff his rational and true self always wanted. And so, Tony set off, on foot, to explore the world. Meet all sorts of people, get into so many ‘things’ and experience humans at their natural state, that is, going about their lives.

What’s so incredible about this is, on the surface, people can’t call what he did fun or exciting. Hell, I’m pretty sure he wasn’t so high on life the time he ate the eye of a seal, RAW. Or the time he piggybacked a tuk-tuk in Congo, a literal war-zone. But when you see past his antics, you’ll find a man, like you and me, just talking to people. Being curious, immersing in someone else’s culture and becoming one with someone else’s way of life.

You’ll note that most travel was on foot. Think about that for a moment, walking is the simplest form of moving there is. But all he experienced came to him because of this. And recently, as I’m about a month in Singapore, all travel I’ve done is on foot too. I’m vegetarian, so I can't really taste all the food. But talking to people reveals a starkly different story than that told on the television or on reels.

I’ve met Americans, Italians, Spaniards, Fillipinos and every single one of them I’ve loved talking to. Travelling on foot reveals a very human image of the world. You can connect to people who you can’t ever dream of even relating to at a fundamental level. Flirting with the cashier, clicking photos together at the Jewel, chatting with the couple sitting next to you in the cafe.

Not even one of them cared who I was, nor I for them. But talking and being curious is human nature. Flirting playfully, gaping at the same fireworks. It’s as if the world reveals itself when you travel on foot, love by heart and think in the present. I think we all have to do that nowadays, to dim out the chaos that’s totally becoming our world.
