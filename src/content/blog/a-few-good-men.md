"A house divided against itself cannot stand"

On a simple day in Springfield, Illinois; Abraham Lincoln, then president-elect of the United States of America, said these words to a convening crowd in 1858, in his famous ‘A house divided’ speech. While this does not serve much purpose in this passage, it serves to show how brilliant of a man Lincoln was, and how his words moved sentiments in a crowd in the most banal way.

In American history, there have been many such similar enigmatic figures, presidents especially. Another example is Theodore ‘Bull Moose’ Roosevelt. A small burly man, Teddy Roosevelt is perhaps the most American an American can be. In 1912, he survived an assassination attempt in Wisconsin. The story goes that the bullet, fired at short range directly at the torso, was slowed by a 50 page folded speech and got lodged in a metal glass case. Even though some parts of his ribs were broken, he delivered the entire speech unbothered, famously saying ‘it takes more than that to kill a bull moose’ and went to arrest the assailant himself, even lecturing the man about his poor aim.

But I’m writing today not to yap about my admiration for these men. But to highlight my admiration for this archetype of man. This idea of men devoting their entire existence to something much bigger than themselves. Giving their lives for an ideal, stoic and principled. This degradation of the cogito has ushered in an era of ‘soft people’. Hell, I know friends who get nervous around women for absolutely no reason. Back then, we had men standing against entire armies, even when death was certain [The Famous 300 Spartans]. The unspoken extinction of spirit has been so silent, it’s possible to connect most, if not all, problems facing society to it.

The ongoing US-Iran war, the genocidal nature of Israeli expansion, the russian aggression against Ukraine, the scandals coming out against world leaders. All of this is fed to the upcoming generation, their idols are false, the people they’re closest to are decadent. They’re inheriting principles from a facade. What’s more, their broken spirit is set up to face even more dangerous problems that are also inherited! The job crisis, AI and its implications, housing and water shortage.

The bottomline is this, if we can’t solve our problems, we’ll most certainly hand them to our young. But not giving them the indomitable spirit that I was referring to in a bygone era is the final humiliation of mankind. We can lose everything we call ourselves, nothing is truly ours except the purity and sincerity of our souls. Now it is our duty to teach our children at least that. We must be better, for the sake of our children.
